Synapse builds a seeded, connected mesh once during preparation: 1,700 junctions on desktop and 680 on mobile. Local connections and a few longer bridges create dense patches and open space. Four graph-distance fields let detected attacks propagate along strand lengths, with at most eight overlapping impulses. Shared GPU deformation keeps endpoints attached. Held loud notes and silence cannot repeatedly trigger pulses; track changes clear signals. Frozen playback clocks hold travel and signal age. Reduced motion holds signals and suppresses deformation; switching away suspends the signal timeline without catch-up. Focus varies with camera depth using soft analytic ribbons and point halos, rather than a full photographic lens simulation. Desktop adds a fine secondary layer of bowed strands; mobile omits it and uses one segment per connection. Both retain artwork palette tinting, frequency lights, camera controls and adaptive resolution. No reference bitmap, extra audio analysis or network service is needed.

Orrery uses a dedicated renderer with nine textured bodies, thin antialiased paths and a sparse stellar background. Most orbits share a tilted plane; four inclined paths open the silhouette. All travel follows the existing integrated world clocks, so changing musical energy never multiplies absolute time or teleports planets. Frozen clocks hold orbital travel, reduced motion suppresses geometric deformation, and the existing camera moves through the field. The scene is an artistic astronomical study, not a physical orbital simulation. Desktop draws 26,000 dust points and 320 segments per path; mobile draws 11,000 and 192. It shares the original analysis and requires no downloaded imagery.

Contour uses its own screen-space renderer, independent of the camera, perspective and kaleidoscope. Portrait layouts keep lettering upright and arrange the gesture vertically. Three geometric brush studies alternate over 17-second painting cycles; old ink dissolves as the new stroke is drawn. Distortion follows the existing bass, melody and spectrum envelopes continuously. Rising audible accents deposit at most eight active bursts (192 small droplets); held loud tones and silence cannot repeatedly spray new marks. Marks spread and dry, and the timeline stops when playback is paused. Track changes clear old marks. Inactive scenes discard missing time. Reduced motion holds the painting timeline and suppresses deformation, with only the small frequency strip and pigment response remaining live. Lettering is rasterized only on a layout change, using installed system fonts. The seal is an original geometric mark, not invented calligraphy. Mobile uses 36 main bristles and 112 path segments instead of 64 and 192.

Aerial has 24 main bodies on desktop and 16 on mobile, with four central liquid lobes and up to 277/99 small satellite beads respectively. Physics advances at 120 fixed steps per second with interpolated positions. Solid bodies exchange mass-weighted collision impulses and bounce against a floor. The central lobes share a smooth implicit surface, visibly merging and separating as their centres move. **Liquid merging** at zero makes all main bodies collide as solids; above zero it controls the central surface's neck width. **Studio darkness** changes the backdrop, floor and reflected environment from pale to dark. Both settings are saved and can be reset independently.

Broad rectangular light cards, a cool rim, inter-body shadowing, floor contact shadows and a subdued floor reflection establish the studio. Materials include porcelain, pearl, tinted chrome and glass. The liquid is an artistic smooth-union surface, not a volume-conserving fluid solver; glass uses an approximate single transmitted ray and the floor uses inexpensive sphere reflections. These choices keep the scene bounded on phones. Movement and Beat motion control the simulation; Living detail, Orbiting dust and Particle size control the smaller beads. Frequency lights and Musical flashes remain independent.

Aerial stays upright in portrait. Manual and rail cameras are limited above the floor, with unlimited horizontal orbit and no accumulated vertical overscroll. Inactive scenes suspend physics; long gaps are discarded. Paused/frozen playback holds the clock, reduced motion holds the simulation, and changing songs resets accumulated motion and attack state. Percussion uses an armed threshold and cooldown so a held loud note does not continuously kick every body.

Flux rotates around the centre of its history volume. Antialiased, camera-facing filament ribbons retain their width at side angles; crosswise threads and a mirrored undercurrent keep the side and underside legible. Older samples retain a faint presence for rear views. Tiny pixel glints and depth-dependent particle softness add detail without a full-screen blur. Sparse world-space motes and a camera-aware haze occupy the surrounding negative space; **Orbiting dust** controls the motes, including off. Flux uses gentler camera zoom to keep its volume in view at large sculpture scales. Its atmosphere and spectral light are combined before tone mapping to preserve dark-space detail.

Flux keeps approximately 4.2 seconds of the existing 48-band analysis in a 6 KB memory buffer. It is an artistic spectrogram, not a calibrated measurement display. No audio is recorded or uploaded, and no model is installed. History stops while paused or frozen, clears for a different song, and does no hidden catch-up. Silence drains the existing trail instead of inventing new peaks. Reduced motion holds the history and flowing motion still. Mobile uses 28 transverse and 12 longitudinal traces instead of 56 and 24, fewer wave particles, and at most 630 surrounding motes instead of 1,950. The existing adaptive resolution and rotated portrait wave composition remain in place. `tests/flux-render.cjs` reviews front, quarter, side, rear, overhead, underside, close and phone views using generated frequency envelopes; it also checks finite radiance, paused state, empty silent history and the dust control.

Afterglow replaces Glasshouse. A saved Glasshouse selection opens Afterglow automatically. No artwork or additional audio analysis is downloaded. Noctiluca is now a dedicated open-water renderer; its earlier seabed, grass, mantas, sharks and jellyfish are no longer drawn.

The directions share playback, musical analysis, cover colors, reduced motion and frequency-light settings. Materials interpret those inputs differently: Contour absorbs light as ink; the neon drive remaps artwork colors toward saturated neon. Scenic backgrounds stay composed while the existing camera moves the foreground geometry; Contour is a fully flat exception.

Switching fades out the current composition before revealing the next. Repeated choices settle on the last selection. A settings transfer includes the selected art direction; device-specific rendering quality remains local.

## Automatic ribbon forms

The manual Ribbon shape picker has been removed on both platforms. Old manual choices are discarded on loading settings. Shapes continue to follow saved track character and musical chapters, including **Lorenz butterfly, Rössler current, Figure-eight knot, Cinquefoil weave, Hopf garland, and Enneper flower**, alongside the existing Möbius current and other forms.

These are musical sculptures inspired by mathematical forms, not scientific visualizations. Attractor trajectories are integrated once during preparation; live frames sample the cached curves on the GPU.

## Review and safeguards

The latest Contour review replaced the nearly static circles with an independent painting scene. Render iterations corrected wire-like dry marks, text collisions, sharp folds under strong spectral deformation, overly bright red, and clipped portrait geometry. The material remains a procedural interpretation of ink rather than a fluid or paper-absorption simulation.

The owner judged the first four alternatives substantially below the original. That feedback supersedes the earlier optimistic assessment. This revision changes the scene concepts, geometry and musical behavior rather than simply adjusting brightness. Internal render reviews corrected closed manta-wing tips, excessive comet highlights, correlated ink-drop trails and overly regular aquarium placement. The owner’s later geometric reference redirected Contour toward intersecting circles, open paper and a red center, replacing the literal landscape and bamboo. Later brush references inform pressure variations, bristle trails and pale edge washes. The latest Orrery references supersede the clockwork direction: all gears, dial markings and solid brass bands have been removed.

Review criteria are distinct art direction, recognizable silhouettes, layered composition, continuous motion, musical correspondence, safe switching, accessibility and rendering cost. A target rating is not evidence of artistic parity with Resonance. The remaining artistic judgment needs listening across varied recordings, particularly creature movement and whether the geometric Contour composition stays expressive during quieter passages.

Automated checks cover saved-style migration, removal of manual shape choices, rapid switching, reduced motion, continuous travel under changing envelopes, all automatic forms, actual GPU output, independent bass/melody/percussion responses, zero frequency-light input, fixed-frame stability and graphics-context recovery. Isolated Windows and Android interface checks cover persistence, keyboard focus and compact layouts. Full application regression, startup, queue and native Android checks are required before publication.

Rendering reuses the existing canvas and bloom pipeline. Alternate scenes skip the original particle birth/flow simulation. Noctiluca simulates 384 leaders on desktop and 160 on mobile at 30 fixed steps per second, with interpolated positions and a spatial grid for local separation, alignment and cohesion. GPU followers extend this to at most 4,608 desktop fish and 1,280 phone fish; the saved Living detail setting changes this bounded population. Fish have a three-dimensional body, forked tail, dorsal/pectoral fins and velocity-aligned orientation. Tail phase follows integrated swim time, avoiding jumps when musical energy changes. School opening moves the flock between compact and open formations, while gated attacks push local escape pockets outward. A held loud note does not repeatedly scatter the fish. Track changes reset the flock; pause and reduced motion hold it, and inactive scenes do no hidden catch-up.

Noctiluca shares a submerged perspective camera across the fish, surface and water. It stays upright on phones. There is no seabed or horizon geometry. RGB absorption, bounded integration through moving surface illumination, silver glints and marine snow establish depth. Sun shafts is a saved, resettable light control; melody and percussion also affect illumination. Particle size, Sculpture scale, Movement, Beat motion, Turbulence, Living detail, Orbiting dust, palette and frequency lights remain connected. Surface light, scattering and fish anatomy are procedural artistic approximations, not a full optical or biological simulation; fish do not cast volumetric shadows into the shafts. Mobile uses fewer leaders, followers, mesh segments and ray samples.

Surfaces are bounded analytic meshes with shared depth testing. Motion clocks integrate changes in speed instead of multiplying absolute time by changing audio envelopes, avoiding position jumps on attacks.

Phone quality and adaptive resolution are unchanged. Desktop GPU measurements at phone rendering resolution compare paths but do not establish battery use, heat or sustained frame rate on a physical phone.

## Trial checklist

- Compare quiet acoustic music, dense electronic music, vocals and percussion.
- Watch silhouettes and continuity for several minutes, including quiet-to-loud transitions.
- Switch directions while playing, paused and using desktop audio.
- Adjust Frequency lights intensity/contrast, then reset them.
- Check controls, lyrics and overlays against the pale paper scene.
- On Android, compare sustained smoothness and heat with Resonance.

The approved channel and website remain unchanged until this development batch is approved.
