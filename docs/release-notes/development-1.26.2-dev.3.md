# Synapse — physical audio response

Windows **1.26.2-dev.3** and Android **0.26.2-dev.3** (versionCode **109**), isolated development channel.

- Particle movement now has a separate physical audio response. Loudness directly drives a three-dimensional morph of actual positions; bass and percussion add spring velocity impulses. The existing independent regional flow remains underneath, so the result bends locally without returning to uniform expansion or radial shockwaves.
- The displacement curve responds to ordinary low spectrum magnitudes instead of requiring near-full-scale bands. Impulses, velocity and morph amplitude are bounded. Movement, turbulence and beat-motion controls affect the response, and held notes do not repeatedly launch attacks.
- Frequency is mapped diagonally through width, height and depth. Junctions, threads and facets share that spatial frequency identity, and the gradient uses the current music palette. Low and high notes animate their corresponding parts of the volume rather than a random collection of points.
- Bass releases small bursts of three to ten drifting points from low-frequency junctions. They fade in and out over short lifetimes; active particles are not overwritten when the bounded pool is full. The phone uses at most 48 burst particles, desktop 144, alongside the unchanged junction/current budgets.
- A separate low-percussion transient detector catches repeated bass hits when the broad onset envelope stays high between beats. Detection follows the recent local energy peak, so ordinary music levels can drive both physical kicks and connected light currents.
- Ambient specks also move with their local frequency energy. Existing connected light currents, palette transitions, smooth pause decay, hold/reduced-motion behaviour and banding-free background remain.

Verification includes direct comparisons of loud and quiet particle positions with identical flow clocks, realistic low-level spectrum response, localized bass velocity, the 3D gradient, burst lifetimes, held-note suppression, continuity, bounds and pause decay. GPU checks measure actual displacement and active bursts as well as lighting, camera angles, palettes, phone layout and gradient quality. A local preview uses generated music passed through Resonance's actual offline FFT/role analysis, with synchronized sound.

Verification: all **570 shared tests**, **151 Android JVM tests**, seven-world GPU checks and graphics-context recovery pass. Both ABI builds, the device-test compilation and Android lint pass (0 errors; 9 existing warnings). The focused GPU check confirms actual audio displacement, bounded bass bursts, changing nearest connections, smooth pause decay, held/reduced motion, camera views, music palettes and a smooth background gradient. In the 22-second preview, repeated bursts stop through the quiet passage and resume with the bass. Both extracted packages pass the complete nested startup-module graph; an isolated empty-library Windows build reaches the welcome screen. Android retains the existing signing certificate, package ID and ARM64 ABI. Legacy updater compatibility, uploaded hashes and anonymous channel isolation are checked before completing publication. Automated phone layouts are desktop simulations; physical Android testing remains outstanding. Main and ordinary releases remain Windows 1.26.1 / Android 0.26.1-preview pending approval.
